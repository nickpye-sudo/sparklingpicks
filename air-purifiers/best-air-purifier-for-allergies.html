What Allergy Sufferers Should Look For

True HEPA vs. "HEPA-Type"

This matters. True HEPA filters must capture 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns and larger—that includes pollen (10-100μm), dust mite allergens (1-10μm), mold spores (3-40μm), and pet dander (2.5-10μm). "HEPA-type" or "HEPA-style" filters have no regulated standard and may miss 50%+ of allergens.

Room Coverage (Be Realistic)

Manufacturers exaggerate. A purifier rated for "500 sq ft" might only clean that space once per hour—allergy relief needs 4-6 air changes per hour. For a 500 sq ft room, get a purifier rated for 1,000+ sq ft.

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)

The only standardized measure of purifier performance. Look for CADR scores of 200+ for bedrooms, 300+ for living rooms. Higher is better.

Noise Levels

You'll run it 24/7. If it's loud, you'll turn it off—defeating the purpose. Look for purifiers with sleep modes under 30 dB.

Filter Replacement Costs

The real cost of air purifiers is filters. A "$100 purifier" with $60 filters every 6 months costs more than a "$300 purifier" with $50 filters annually. Calculate 3-year total cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until I notice allergy relief?

Most people report improvement within 24-48 hours. For maximum benefit, run the purifier continuously on auto mode. The air in your room should cycle through the filter 4-6 times per hour.

Should I run it 24/7?

Yes. Allergens constantly enter your home and get stirred up by movement. Turning off your purifier lets allergens accumulate. Modern purifiers with eco modes use minimal power when air is clean.

One purifier or multiple?

For whole-home coverage, you'll likely need 2-3 purifiers unless you have a very large model. Prioritize bedrooms first—you spend 8 hours there daily.

Do air purifiers help with dust mite allergies?

Yes, but with caveats. Airborne dust mite allergens (from disturbed bedding, walking on carpet) are captured by HEPA. However, most dust mite allergens live in bedding and carpet—you also need allergen-proof mattress covers and regular vacuuming with a HEPA vacuum.

What about ozone?

Avoid air purifiers that generate ozone—it's a lung irritant that can worsen allergies and asthma. All purifiers on this page either produce no ozone (HEPA-only) or produce trace amounts well below California's strict limits (0.05 ppm). We've marked any models with ionization features.
