Buying Guide: Air Purifiers for Mold

What Actually Captures Mold Spores?

Mold spores range from 3-40 microns — well within the capture range of True HEPA filters (99.97% of particles ≥0.3 microns). Any True HEPA or H13 HEPA filter will capture mold spores effectively. The real question is how much air does it move and can it handle the odors.

Why Carbon Matters for Mold

Mold doesn't just release spores — it produces MVOCs (microbial volatile organic compounds) that create that musty, mildewy smell. HEPA filters don't capture gases. You need activated carbon to absorb these odors. More carbon = better odor absorption. The Austin Air HealthMate's 15 lbs of carbon is exceptional; most budget units have 1-2 lbs.

Sizing for Mold-Prone Spaces

For mold control, you want 4-6 air changes per hour (ACH) — more than typical use. Calculate: Room volume (L × W × H) × 4 ÷ 60 = minimum CFM needed. For a 400 sq ft basement with 8' ceilings, that's 3,200 cubic feet × 4 ÷ 60 = 213 CFM minimum.

Pair with a Dehumidifier

Air purifiers capture airborne spores but don't address moisture — the root cause of mold growth. For basements and bathrooms, run a dehumidifier to keep humidity below 50%. The air purifier cleans the air while the dehumidifier prevents new growth.

Avoid These Technologies for Mold

Ionizers alone don't capture spores — they make particles stick to surfaces (where mold can still grow). Ozone generators are not safe for occupied spaces. UV-C lights may kill some spores but don't remove them from the air. Stick with True HEPA + carbon for mold.

When Air Purifiers Aren't Enough

If you see visible mold growth, have water damage, or smell persistent mustiness, you may need professional remediation first. Air purifiers maintain air quality but can't fix active mold colonies. Address the moisture source, remediate visible mold, then use an air purifier for ongoing air quality maintenance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can air purifiers kill mold?
Air purifiers with HEPA filters capture airborne mold spores but don't "kill" them — they remove them from the air you breathe. Some purifiers with UV-C lights claim to kill captured spores, but the main benefit is simply removing spores from circulation. To eliminate mold, you need to address the moisture source and remediate visible growth.
Do I need a HEPA filter for mold, or will any filter work?
True HEPA or H13 HEPA filters are essential for mold spores. Standard filters, electrostatic filters, or "HEPA-type" filters may not capture the full range of mold spore sizes (3-40 microns). Look for "True HEPA" or "H13 HEPA" certification. Avoid units that only say "HEPA-style" or "HEPA-like."
How big of an air purifier do I need for my basement?
For basements, aim for 4-6 air changes per hour. Measure your basement's square footage and ceiling height, then look for a purifier rated for that space. A 500 sq ft basement with 8' ceilings (4,000 cubic feet) needs at least 267 CFM for 4 air changes per hour. When in doubt, size up — a larger purifier running on low is quieter and more effective than a small one on high.
Should I run my air purifier 24/7 for mold?
Yes, for mold-prone areas, continuous operation is recommended. Mold spores are constantly released, and stopping the purifier allows them to accumulate. Use auto mode if available — it adjusts fan speed based on detected particle levels. Running 24/7 on low-medium costs $15-30/year in electricity for most units.
