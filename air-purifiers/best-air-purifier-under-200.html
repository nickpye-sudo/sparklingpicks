Budget Air Purifier Buying Guide

What to Expect Under $200

Budget air purifiers deliver the same HEPA filtration as premium models — they capture 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns and larger. The trade-offs are coverage area (150-400 sq ft vs 800+ on premium models), noise levels, and smart features. For a bedroom or home office, budget models work great.

Key Specs to Compare

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate): Higher is better. Look for 100+ CFM for small rooms, 200+ CFM for medium rooms. CADR tells you how quickly the purifier cleans the air.

Coverage area: Match this to your actual room size. Going bigger is fine; going smaller means inadequate filtration.

Noise levels: 20-30 dB is whisper-quiet (good for sleep). 40-50 dB is white noise level. Above 50 dB is noticeable.

Smart Features Worth Paying For

Auto mode (adjusts fan speed based on air quality) is the most useful smart feature — it means you can set-and-forget. App control is nice for scheduling and monitoring, but not essential. Voice control via Alexa/Google is convenient if you're already in that ecosystem.

Ongoing Costs

Factor in filter replacement costs when comparing. Budget purifiers need new filters every 6-12 months. Levoit filters run $15-25, while Winix and Blueair filters cost $30-50. Over 3 years, filter costs can exceed the purifier price.

When to Spend More

Consider upgrading to a $250-400 purifier (like the Coway Airmega AP-1512HH) if you need: coverage over 400 sq ft, true air quality monitoring, lower ongoing filter costs, or you're managing serious allergies or asthma.
