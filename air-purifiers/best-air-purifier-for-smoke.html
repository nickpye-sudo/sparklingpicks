How to Choose an Air Purifier for Smoke

Why Smoke is Hard to Filter

Smoke contains both particles (captured by HEPA) and gases/VOCs (captured by activated carbon). Most air purifiers focus on particles and have minimal carbon — fine for dust and pollen, inadequate for smoke. For smoke removal, you need both strong HEPA filtration AND substantial activated carbon.

How Much Activated Carbon Do You Need?

  • Light smoke (occasional cooking, distant wildfires): 1-2 lbs carbon is sufficient
  • Moderate smoke (indoor smoking, nearby wildfires): 3-5 lbs carbon recommended
  • Heavy smoke (active wildfire season, heavy smoker): 10+ lbs carbon needed — Austin Air is your best option

CADR Ratings Explained

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) measures how quickly a purifier cleans air of specific pollutants. For smoke, look for CADR ratings of 200+ for bedrooms, 300+ for living rooms, and 400+ for large open spaces. Multiply CADR by 1.55 to get the recommended room size in square feet.

Types of Smoke & Which Purifiers Work Best

  • Wildfire smoke: Austin Air HealthMate (massive carbon) or IQAir (ultrafine particles)
  • Cigarette/cigar smoke: Austin Air or Coway Airmega 400 (need both particle + odor control)
  • Cooking smoke: Any True HEPA with activated carbon — Levoit Core 400S is budget-friendly
  • Fireplace/wood smoke: Austin Air or high-carbon options

For severe wildfire smoke, consider running multiple purifiers on different floors or in key rooms (bedroom, home office). A budget unit like the Levoit Core 400S in bedrooms plus an Austin Air in the main living area provides comprehensive coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do HEPA filters remove smoke?
HEPA filters capture smoke particles down to 0.3 microns — including most wildfire and tobacco smoke particles. However, HEPA alone doesn't remove smoke odors or VOCs (gases). For complete smoke removal, you need both HEPA + activated carbon filtration. Look for purifiers with at least 2+ lbs of carbon for moderate smoke, or 10+ lbs for heavy smoke.
How much carbon do I need for wildfire smoke?
For occasional wildfire smoke (AQI 100-150), 2-5 lbs of activated carbon is usually sufficient. For prolonged exposure or severe smoke events (AQI 200+), you'll want 10+ lbs of carbon — the Austin Air HealthMate with 15 lbs is the gold standard. Most budget purifiers have less than 1 lb, which saturates quickly during active fires.
Can air purifiers remove cigarette smoke smell?
Yes, but only if they have substantial activated carbon. HEPA filters capture the particles but do nothing for the odor. The Austin Air HealthMate (15 lbs carbon) is our top pick for cigarette smoke. Budget options with 1-2 lbs of carbon will reduce smell but not eliminate it in a smoker's home.
Should I keep windows closed when using an air purifier for smoke?
Yes — close all windows and doors when outdoor air quality is poor. Air purifiers work best in sealed spaces. If you need fresh air, wait for low-smoke periods (usually early morning) and ventilate briefly. Consider a window filter or HRV system for ongoing fresh air during extended wildfire seasons.
