What RV Camping Requires

🚐 Typical RV Power Needs

  • 12V RV fridge: 40-60W (960-1,440Wh/day)
  • Lighting: 20-50W
  • Phone/tablet charging: 20W
  • Laptop: 50W
  • Fan: 30-50W
  • CPAP: 30-60W overnight
  • Coffee maker: 600-900W (brief use)

Rough daily total: 1,000-2,000Wh depending on usage

For extended off-grid stays, you need at least 1,500Wh capacity plus solar charging to replenish daily.

Why Solar Input Matters

☀️ Solar Charging Math

A 200W solar panel produces ~800-1,000Wh per day (4-5 hours of good sun).

To replenish 1,500Wh daily, you need:

  • 400W of solar panels (typical RV roof setup)
  • Power station with 400W+ solar input

This is why the Bluetti AC200MAX (900W solar input) is ideal — it can handle serious solar setups.

RV-Specific Features to Look For

  • High solar input: 400W+ for serious off-grid use
  • LiFePO4 batteries: Handle temperature extremes better
  • 30A RV outlet: Powers RV appliances directly (some models have this)
  • Expandability: Add batteries as needs grow
  • Rugged build: Road vibration is brutal on electronics

Power Station vs. RV's Built-in System

Many RVs have built-in battery banks and inverters. Why add a portable power station?

  • ✅ Portability: Take it outside, to a tent, or into your home
  • ✅ Redundancy: Backup if RV system fails
  • ✅ Better solar integration: Often easier than modifying RV wiring
  • ✅ Upgradeable: Swap for newer tech without rewiring RV

Solar Panel Pairings

Most power station brands sell matching solar panels:

  • Bluetti PV200: 200W panel, $350, pairs with AC200MAX
  • Jackery SolarSaga 200: 200W panel, $500, pairs with Explorer 2000 Plus
  • EcoFlow 400W: High-output folding panel, $800, pairs with Delta 2 Max

For RV roof mounting, you can also use rigid panels from Renogy or Rich Solar — just check voltage compatibility with your power station.
