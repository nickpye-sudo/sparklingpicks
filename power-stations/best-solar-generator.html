How Solar Generators Work

A solar generator is really just two components designed to work together:

  1. Solar panel — Converts sunlight into DC electricity
  2. Power station — Stores that electricity in a battery and converts it to AC power for your devices

The key specs that matter:

  • Panel wattage — How fast it generates power (200W panel = 200 watts per hour in ideal sun)
  • Station capacity — How much it stores (1,000Wh = 1,000 watts for 1 hour, or 100 watts for 10 hours)
  • Solar input max — How much solar power the station can accept (some stations can take multiple panels)

Real-world solar charging is about 80% of rated capacity due to angle, clouds, and temperature. A 200W panel typically delivers 150-180W in good conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a solar generator power a house?

Partially. A 2,000Wh+ unit can run essentials (fridge, lights, phones, Wi-Fi, medical devices) for 12-24 hours. For whole-home backup, you'd need the EcoFlow Delta Pro with expansion batteries.

How long do solar generators last?

LiFePO4 batteries last 3,000-4,000 cycles (10-15 years of regular use). Lithium-ion lasts 500-800 cycles (2-4 years). Always check the battery type.

Can I charge while using the solar generator?

Yes, all modern units support pass-through charging. You can solar charge and power devices simultaneously.

What's the difference between a solar generator and a gas generator?

Solar generators: Silent, no fumes, no fuel, indoor-safe, zero maintenance, limited power. Gas generators: Loud, toxic exhaust, requires fuel, outdoor-only, unlimited runtime. Solar is better for most uses; gas is better for multi-day high-power needs.

Kit vs buying separately — which is cheaper?

Kits typically save 10-15% over buying the same components separately. They're also guaranteed compatible, which matters for solar input connections.
