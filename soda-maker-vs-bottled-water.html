Soda Maker vs Bottled Water

Which is actually cheaper? We do the math.

📅 Updated Feb 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

A soda maker requires upfront investment. Bottled water has no upfront cost but adds up over time. Which is actually cheaper? Let's run the numbers.

The Short Answer

A soda maker saves 75-80% compared to bottled sparkling water. A $90 SodaStream pays for itself in 3-4 months for regular drinkers, then saves $300-500+ per year ongoing.

Break-Even Point

When Does a Soda Maker Pay for Itself?

  • vs Store brand: ~180 liters (3-4 months @ 10L/week)

  • vs Perrier: ~50 liters (5-6 weeks)

Beyond Cost: Other Factors

Convenience

  • Soda maker: Always available, no shopping trips, no hauling bottles
  • Bottled: Runs out, requires regular shopping, heavy to carry
  • Winner: Soda maker

Environmental Impact

  • Soda maker: One reusable bottle, recyclable CO2 cylinders
  • Bottled: Hundreds of plastic bottles per year
  • Winner: Soda maker (by a huge margin)

Taste

  • Soda maker: Fresh carbonation, adjustable fizz levels
  • Bottled: Consistent, but can go flat
  • Winner: Tie (personal preference)

Storage Space

  • Soda maker: Small countertop footprint + spare cylinder
  • Bottled: Bulk storage of cases
  • Winner: Soda maker

The Verdict

Soda Maker Wins on Cost

A soda maker is cheaper than bottled sparkling water in virtually every scenario. The only exception: extremely light drinkers (less than 2-3 liters per week) might not recoup the upfront cost quickly enough for it to make sense.

For anyone drinking 5+ liters per week, a soda maker is an obvious choice financially — plus you get convenience and environmental benefits.
