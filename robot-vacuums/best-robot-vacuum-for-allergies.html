Why HEPA Matters for Allergies

Most robot vacuums advertise "HEPA-style" or "high-efficiency" filters. These are marketing terms that mean nothing. True HEPA filters capture 99.97% of particles as small as 0.3 microns — including:

  • Dust mites (10-300 microns)
  • Pet dander (0.5-100 microns)
  • Pollen (10-100 microns)
  • Mold spores (2-100 microns)
  • Bacteria (0.3-60 microns)

But the filter is only half the battle. If the vacuum body isn't fully sealed, allergens escape through gaps and seams — defeating the entire purpose. All our picks have sealed airpaths that force 100% of air through the HEPA filter.

💡 Pro Tip: Self-Emptying is Non-Negotiable

Emptying a dustbin releases a cloud of allergens directly into your breathing space. Self-emptying bases with sealed bags (like AllergenLock) keep debris contained for 30-60 days. This single feature makes the biggest difference for allergy sufferers.

Buying Guide: Allergies Edition

How Often Should Allergy Sufferers Vacuum?

Daily. Seriously. This is why robot vacuums are perfect for allergies — they can clean every day without requiring your time. Set it to run when you leave for work and come home to cleaner air.

Bagged vs Bagless Self-Emptying

For allergies, bagged is better. AllergenLock bags (Roomba) seal debris inside. Bagless bins (most other brands) require opening and dumping, which releases allergens. If you go bagless, empty it outside.

Replace Filters Regularly

HEPA filters lose effectiveness over time. Replace every 2-3 months for optimal allergen capture. Both Roomba models use the same filter, around $25 for a 3-pack.

Air Purifiers + Robot Vacuums = Best Combo

Robot vacuums capture settled allergens. Air purifiers capture airborne allergens. Together, they cover both vectors. Run your robot vacuum first, then let your air purifier clear what gets stirred up.
