How to Choose a Robot Vacuum for Hardwood

Rubber vs. Bristle Rollers

Bristle brushes can trap small debris and drag it across your floor, causing micro-scratches over time. Rubber extractors (like those on Roborock and Roomba) flex around debris and release it without scratching. This is non-negotiable for hardwood.

Suction Power Matters Less

On carpet, high suction extracts embedded dirt. On hardwood, you just need enough airflow to lift surface dust and debris. 2500-4000Pa is plenty. Higher suction can actually blow light debris around instead of picking it up. Look for robots with automatic suction adjustment.

Mopping: Worth It or Not?

For sealed hardwood (polyurethane finish), robot mops are excellent for maintaining that streak-free shine between deep cleans. For oiled or waxed hardwood, avoid water-based mopping — stick to vacuum-only robots like the Roomba j7+.

Navigation Quality

LiDAR and camera-based navigation (found on all our picks) prevents the robot from bumping repeatedly into furniture legs and baseboards. Budget robots with random bump-navigation can scuff paint and damage trim over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a robot vacuum scratch my hardwood floors?

Not if you choose one with rubber rollers. All four robots on this page use rubber extractors instead of bristle brushes, which prevents scratching. Avoid budget robots with stiff bristle brushes.

Can I use a robot mop on hardwood?

Yes, on sealed hardwood with polyurethane finish. Avoid robot mops on waxed, oiled, or unsealed hardwood floors. When in doubt, check with your flooring manufacturer.

How often should I run my robot vacuum on hardwood?

Daily or every other day for high-traffic areas. Hardwood shows dust and debris more than carpet, so frequent light cleaning looks better than weekly deep cleaning.
