Why Self-Emptying Matters

Standard robot vacuums need emptying every 1-3 cleaning cycles. Self-emptying models use a base station that automatically sucks debris from the vacuum's dustbin into a larger bag or bin. Most can go 30-60 days without any intervention.

This matters because the #1 reason people stop using robot vacuums is maintenance fatigue. With self-emptying, you set it and genuinely forget it.

💡 What We Look For

  • Base capacity: Bigger bags = less frequent changes
  • Suction transfer: How completely does it empty the vacuum?
  • Noise during empty: Some bases are quieter than others
  • Bag vs bagless: Bags are cleaner; bagless saves money

How to Choose

1. Do you need mopping?

If you have mostly hard floors and want both vacuuming and mopping handled automatically, look at the Roborock S8 Pro Ultra or Dreame L20 Ultra. If you only need vacuuming, the Roomba j7+ offers better value.

2. How much suction do you need?

For homes with multiple pets or thick carpet, prioritize suction: Roomba s9+ or Dreame L20 Ultra. For average homes with mixed flooring, standard suction (Roomba j7+, Shark AI Ultra) is plenty.

3. Bagged vs bagless?

Bagged (Roomba): Cleaner emptying, traps allergens, but bags cost ~$7 each.
Bagless (Shark, Roborock, Dreame): No ongoing costs, but emptying can release dust.

4. What's your budget?

  • Under $500: Shark AI Ultra ($449)
  • $500-$700: Roomba j7+ ($599) — our pick for most people
  • $1,000+: Roomba s9+ for suction, Roborock/Dreame for all-in-one
