What Makes a Robot Vacuum Good for Large Homes?

After extensive testing, we identified four critical factors:

  1. Battery life (120+ minutes minimum): Most homes over 2,500 sq ft require at least 90 minutes of continuous cleaning. We recommend 120+ minutes for margin.
  2. Recharge-and-resume: The vacuum should return to its dock when low, recharge, and continue where it left off—not start over.
  3. Multi-floor mapping: If you have multiple levels, you need a vacuum that saves separate maps and doesn't get confused.
  4. Efficient navigation: Random-bounce vacuums waste battery. LiDAR or camera-based mapping covers more ground per charge.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square feet can a robot vacuum clean?

Mid-range robot vacuums (90-120 min battery) typically cover 1,500-2,500 sq ft. Premium models with 150-180 min batteries can handle 3,000-3,500 sq ft on a single charge. All modern robots with recharge-and-resume can eventually clean any size home, but larger batteries mean fewer interruptions.

Do robot vacuums work for multi-story homes?

Yes, but you'll need to manually move the vacuum between floors. The best models (Roborock S8 Pro Ultra, Dreame L20 Ultra) save separate maps for up to 4 floors and automatically recognize which floor they're on. Budget models may require you to reset the map each time.

Should I get a robot vacuum with a self-emptying base?

For large homes, absolutely. Without self-emptying, you'll need to empty the dustbin every 1-2 cleans. Self-emptying bases hold 30-60 days of debris, which is especially valuable in 3,000+ sq ft homes where the vacuum runs frequently.

Is the Roborock S8 Pro Ultra worth $1,599?

For homes over 2,500 sq ft, yes. The combination of 180-minute runtime, multi-floor mapping, and self-maintaining dock justifies the premium. For smaller homes (under 2,000 sq ft), the Q7 Max+ at $649 offers similar runtime without the premium features.
