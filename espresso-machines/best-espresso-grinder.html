Why the Grinder Matters More Than You Think

Espresso is ruthlessly unforgiving. Unlike drip coffee, a few microns of difference in particle size can mean the difference between a balanced, syrupy shot and bitter mud. A cheap blade grinder creates wildly inconsistent particles — some dust, some boulders — that extract unevenly.

A quality burr grinder gives you consistent particle size, repeatable dosing, and the micro-adjustability needed to dial in your shots day after day. This is why serious home baristas often spend more on their grinder than their machine.

How to Choose the Right Espresso Grinder

Flat vs. Conical Burrs

Flat burrs (Eureka, DF64) produce more uniform particles and what coffee nerds call "clarity" — distinct, bright flavors. Conical burrs (Baratza Sette, most hand grinders) produce a slightly more varied particle distribution, often described as "body" or "sweetness."

In practice, both make excellent espresso. Choose flat if you prefer lighter roasts and bright acidity; choose conical if you like traditional Italian espresso with more body.

Stepped vs. Stepless Adjustment

Stepped grinders (Encore ESP, Sette) have discrete click settings. The Sette 270's 270 steps are so fine you'll never notice. Stepless grinders (Eureka Mignon, JX-Pro) have infinite adjustment via a smooth dial — some prefer the precision, others find it fiddlier.

Retention Matters

Retention is how much coffee stays inside the grinder after dosing. High retention means stale grounds mixing with fresh, affecting flavor. The Sette 270 has near-zero retention; the Eureka Mignon holds 1-2g. For single-dosing (weighing beans before grinding), prioritize low retention.

The Bottom Line

For most home baristas, the Baratza Sette 270 hits the sweet spot of price, performance, and user-friendliness. If noise is a concern, step up to the Eureka Mignon Specialita. On a tight budget, the Baratza Encore ESP or 1Zpresso JX-Pro will get you making genuine espresso without breaking the bank.

Remember: a $200 grinder paired with a $300 machine will produce better espresso than a $100 grinder with a $400 machine. Invest in your grinder first.
