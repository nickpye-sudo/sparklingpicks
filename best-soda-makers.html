What to Look For in a Soda Maker

Before diving into the reviews, here are the key factors we evaluate:

  • CO2 Cylinder Type: Quick-connect (easier) vs screw-in (cheaper refills)
  • Bottle Material: Plastic (durable, cheap) vs glass (premium feel, heavier)
  • Carbonation Strength: Most machines let you control fizz level
  • Build Quality: Plastic vs metal construction
  • Ongoing Costs: CO2 refill prices vary significantly
  • Footprint: Counter space required

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a soda maker worth buying?

Yes, if you drink sparkling water regularly. A soda maker pays for itself in 3-6 months compared to buying bottled sparkling water. You'll also reduce plastic waste significantly. See our full cost analysis.

How much does CO2 cost per litre?

A 60L CO2 cylinder costs £12-15 to exchange, making each litre of sparkling water cost about 20-25p. Compare that to £0.75-1.50 for shop-bought bottles.

SodaStream vs Aarke — which is better?

Functionally they're similar. SodaStream offers better value; Aarke offers premium design. See our detailed SodaStream vs Aarke comparison.

Can I carbonate drinks other than water?

Only with the DrinkMate OmniFizz. SodaStream and Aarke machines are designed for water only — carbonating sugary drinks can cause sticky overflow and void your warranty.

Where can I get CO2 refills?

Argos, Currys, Robert Dyas, Amazon, and some supermarkets offer cylinder exchanges. See our complete CO2 refill guide for best prices.
