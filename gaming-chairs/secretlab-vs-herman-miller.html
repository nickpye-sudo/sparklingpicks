Build Quality & Materials

Secretlab Titan Evo

Secretlab has come a long way from budget gaming chairs. The Titan Evo uses a steel frame, cold-cure foam (denser than standard PU foam), and magnetic armrest toppers that snap into place. The build feels substantial — no creaking or wobbling, even at the max 395 lb capacity.

The upholstery options include SoftWeave fabric (our recommendation for breathability) or NEO Hybrid Leatherette (easier to clean but hotter). The fabric has held up well in our 18-month testing with no visible wear.

Strengths

  • Premium build at mid-range price
  • Cold-cure foam retains shape
  • Multiple size options (S, R, XL)
  • Wide customization (colors, fabrics)

Weaknesses

  • Assembly takes 20–30 minutes
  • Heavy (73 lbs) — hard to move
  • Some report armrest wiggle

Herman Miller Embody Gaming

The Embody is an office chair that Herman Miller adapted for gaming with Logitech. It's engineered differently: instead of foam, it uses a flexible "Pixelated Support" mesh that adapts to your back's micro-movements. The spine-mimicking backrest distributes weight across 150+ pressure points.

Build quality is exceptional — Made in USA with a 12-year warranty that Herman Miller actually honors. The materials are designed for 50,000+ hours of use. This is "buy it for life" territory.

Strengths

  • 12-year warranty (industry best)
  • Mesh prevents heat buildup
  • Designed for 8+ hour sessions
  • Minimal assembly required

Weaknesses

  • $1,695 MSRP is steep
  • No headrest option
  • Limited recline (120° max)
  • Only one size fits all

Ergonomics & Comfort

This is where the price difference starts to make sense — or not, depending on your needs.

For Gaming Sessions (2–4 hours)

Winner: Secretlab Titan Evo

Gaming postures vary. You lean forward in competitive moments, recline during cutscenes, and sometimes just want to kick back. The Titan Evo's 165° recline and 4D armrests accommodate all of this. The magnetic lumbar support snaps to different positions as you shift.

The Herman Miller's limited 120° recline and lack of headrest make it less versatile for casual gaming. It's engineered for one position: sitting upright and working.

For Work Sessions (6–10 hours)

Winner: Herman Miller Embody

Here's where the Embody justifies its price. The dynamic back support moves with you, reducing pressure points and preventing the stiffness that sets in after hour 6 in any foam chair. The mesh keeps you cool during long days.

If your use case is primarily work-from-home with occasional gaming, the Embody will treat your back better over time. If you have existing back issues, it's worth the investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Herman Miller Embody really 4x better than Secretlab?

No — diminishing returns apply. The Embody is maybe 20–30% better for long work sessions, but 4x the price. For most people, Secretlab hits the sweet spot of quality and value.

Can I find the Herman Miller cheaper?

Yes. Herman Miller runs sales (15–20% off), and certified refurbished units from authorized dealers go for $1,100–1,300. The chair is identical, just pre-owned with full warranty.

Does Secretlab make a cheaper option?

The Secretlab Titan Evo Lite ($299) uses less premium materials but keeps the same frame. It's a solid budget option. See our best gaming chairs under $300 guide.

What about the Razer Iskur or other alternatives?

The Razer Iskur V2 ($499) is comparable to Secretlab but with more aggressive lumbar support. The AndaSeat Kaiser 3 ($399) offers similar build quality for less. See our full gaming chair comparison.

Which is better for tall people (6'2"+)?

Secretlab — they offer an XL size for taller/heavier users. The Herman Miller Embody is one-size-fits-all and may not support people over 6'2" as well.
