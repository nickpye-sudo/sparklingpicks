Why Folding E-Bikes?

A folding e-bike isn't just a smaller bike — it's a different lifestyle:

  • Apartment living: No need for a bike room or storage unit. Fold and roll it into your closet.
  • Multi-modal commute: Take it on the train, bus, or subway. Finish the "last mile" on two wheels.
  • RV and van life: Fits inside your vehicle. No exterior rack required (less drag, less theft risk).
  • Travel: Some folding e-bikes fit in airline-approved luggage. Explore cities without rentals.
  • Security: Bring it inside with you. Locks are optional when the bike is under your desk.

Our Testing Process

We evaluated each bike across real-world portability scenarios:

  • Fold time: How fast can you fold/unfold it? Under pressure?
  • Carry weight: Can you actually lift it up stairs? Into a trunk?
  • Folded size: Does it fit in an elevator? Under a desk? In a Honda Civic?
  • Ride quality: Does the smaller frame compromise stability or comfort?
  • Build quality: Do hinges feel solid after 100+ folds?

Folding E-Bike Buying Guide

Weight vs. Portability Trade-off

The biggest decision in folding e-bikes is weight. Lighter bikes are easier to carry but often sacrifice motor power, battery range, or tire size. Consider how you'll actually use the fold:

  • Daily stair carry: Get the lightest option you can afford (Brompton, GoCycle)
  • Car trunk storage: Weight matters less; prioritize ride quality (Lectric, Tern)
  • Rolling into office: Any folder works; weight is secondary

Wheel Size Matters

Smaller wheels fold smaller but ride rougher. Fat tires (3-4") absorb bumps but increase weight and folded size. Most folders use 16" or 20" wheels:

  • 16" wheels: Most compact fold, roughest ride (Brompton)
  • 20" wheels: Good balance of fold size and stability (most folders)
  • 20" fat tires: Smoothest ride but heaviest (Lectric, RadExpand)

Motor Type

Hub motors (in the wheel) are simpler and cheaper. Mid-drive motors (at the pedals) feel more natural and handle hills better but cost more:

  • Hub motor: Budget options, low maintenance (Lectric, Heybike)
  • Mid-drive: Premium feel, better hill climbing (Tern, some GoCycle)
