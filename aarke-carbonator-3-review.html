Design & Build Quality

Let's address the elephant in the room: you're paying $140 extra for how this thing looks. Is that worth it?

If you care about kitchen aesthetics, yes. The Aarke Carbonator 3 is genuinely beautiful in a way that no SodaStream can match. It's designed in Sweden with the same sensibility you'd find in high-end Scandinavian furniture — clean lines, quality materials, understated elegance.

Materials

  • Body: Stainless steel throughout
  • Lever: Metal with smooth action
  • Base: Weighted for stability
  • Bottle: BPA-free PET plastic (not glass)

The lever mechanism deserves special mention. Where SodaStream uses a plastic push-button, Aarke uses a metal lever that feels satisfying to operate. It's the kind of tactile quality you notice every time you make a bottle.

Footprint

The Aarke is surprisingly compact — smaller than most SodaStream models despite looking more substantial. It doesn't dominate the countertop the way you might expect a premium appliance to.

💡 Design Award Winner

The Aarke has won multiple design awards including a Red Dot Design Award. It's regularly featured in interior design publications. This is the soda maker people put on their countertops for photos.

Carbonation Performance

Here's the thing about soda makers: they all work basically the same way. CO2 goes in, fizzy water comes out. The Aarke carbonates exactly as well as a SodaStream — no better, no worse.

The lever mechanism does offer slightly more control. You can feel the carbonation happening and stop at exactly your preferred level. But practically speaking, you'll get the same quality sparkling water from any decent soda maker.

What We Tested

  • Carbonation level: Excellent, on par with SodaStream
  • Consistency: Very consistent across bottles
  • Speed: About 10-15 seconds per bottle
  • Fizz retention: Comparable to SodaStream bottles

One minor advantage: the 800ml bottle means slightly less water per carbonation session, which some people prefer for maximum freshness.

Running Costs

The Aarke uses standard screw-in CO2 cylinders (the kind with a blue cap) — the same ones used by older SodaStream models like the Spirit. This is actually a cost advantage.

Over time, the Aarke's lower per-liter cost offsets some of the upfront premium. If you drink 1,000 liters of sparkling water over 5 years, you'd save about $50 on CO2 versus a quick-connect SodaStream.

Still, it would take decades of use to fully offset the $140 price difference through cylinder savings alone. You're buying this for the design, not the economics.
