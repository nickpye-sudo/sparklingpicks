Standing Desk Buying Guide

Height Range

Your ideal standing height = elbow height when arms are at 90°. Add a few inches for keyboard and monitor. Taller users need desks that go 50"+; shorter users need desks that go below 28".

Stability

3-stage legs > 2-stage legs for stability at full extension. Look for crossbar options and C-frame (rather than T-frame) designs. Test at standing height if possible — typing shouldn't shake your monitor.

Motor Quality

Dual motors are quieter and more powerful than single motors. Look for "continuous duty" or "commercial" ratings if you'll adjust frequently. Motor speed matters less than smooth, quiet operation.

Desktop Material

  • Laminate: Affordable, durable, widest selection
  • Bamboo: Eco-friendly, beautiful, prone to warping if not quality-sourced
  • Solid wood: Premium look, heavyweight, expensive
  • BYOD: Use any desktop you want on a frame-only purchase

FAQ

Are standing desks worth it?

If you sit 8+ hours daily, yes. Studies show alternating sit/stand reduces back pain and increases energy. Most users end up standing 2-4 hours per day, sitting the rest — the flexibility is the real benefit.

How long should I stand?

Start with 30-minute intervals: 30 min standing, 30 min sitting. Gradually increase standing time. Most ergonomics experts recommend 2-4 hours of standing spread throughout the day, not continuous standing.

Do I need an anti-fatigue mat?

Yes, if standing on hard floors. A good mat ($30-60) reduces leg fatigue significantly. Standing on carpet is more forgiving but a mat still helps.

What height should I set for standing?

Elbows at 90° angle when typing, monitor at eye level. Use the desk's memory presets to save your ideal heights for one-button adjustments.
