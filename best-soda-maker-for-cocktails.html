How DrinkMate Handles Non-Water Beverages

Regular soda makers like SodaStream inject CO2 directly into the bottle while it's attached. With water, this is fine. With sugary or acidic beverages, it causes violent fizzing — overflow, mess, potential machine damage.

DrinkMate solves this with a Fizz Infuser system:

  1. Carbonate — CO2 is injected with the bottle attached (same as other machines)
  2. Detach while sealed — bottle disconnects from machine while still under pressure
  3. Slow release — you manually release pressure via a valve, slowly, preventing overflow
  4. Open — once pressure equalizes, open and enjoy

This controlled release is the key innovation. You can let the fizz settle before opening, preventing the geyser effect.

💡 Pro Tips

  • Cold = better fizz — refrigerate beverages before carbonating
  • Less sugar = more fizz — sugary drinks hold carbonation less well
  • Clean after use — sugary residue can clog the Fizz Infuser
  • Don't overfill — leave headroom for expansion

Why You Can't Use SodaStream for Cocktails

SodaStream explicitly warns against carbonating anything but water, and for good reason:

  • No controlled release — removing the bottle immediately releases all pressure
  • Sugar + CO2 = explosion — sugary liquids foam violently when carbonated
  • Warranty voided — damage from non-water carbonation isn't covered
  • Machine damage — sticky liquids can clog valves and damage seals

Can you hack a SodaStream to carbonate other drinks? Some people try, but it's messy, risky, and voids your warranty. The DrinkMate exists specifically to solve this problem — just use the right tool.
