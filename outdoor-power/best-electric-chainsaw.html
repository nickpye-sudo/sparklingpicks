Electric Chainsaw Buying Guide

Battery vs. Corded

Battery (cordless): Go battery if you need portability. Modern 40V-60V saws handle most residential tasks. Main limitation is runtime (30-60 minutes of cutting).

Corded: Choose corded if you work near an outlet and need unlimited runtime. The Oregon CS1500 at $139 delivers 18-inch bar performance without battery costs. Downside: you're tethered to a cord.

Bar Length

  • 12-14 inches: Pruning, light limbing, small firewood
  • 16 inches: The sweet spot for most homeowners. Handles trees up to 12" diameter
  • 18+ inches: For larger trees and more demanding work. Requires more power (56V+)

Voltage/Power

  • 18-20V: Light duty only. Fine for small branches
  • 40V: Good for most residential use
  • 56-60V: Approaches gas performance. Best for serious work

Battery Ecosystem

If you already own cordless tools, check compatibility. Buying into an ecosystem (Ego, DeWalt, Makita, Milwaukee) saves money long-term since batteries are interchangeable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an electric chainsaw cut down trees?

Yes, modern battery chainsaws can fell trees up to 12-16 inches in diameter. For larger trees, you may need to make cuts from multiple angles. Gas saws are still preferred for professional timber work, but for residential tree removal, electric handles it.

How long do electric chainsaw batteries last?

Expect 30-60 minutes of actual cutting time on a full charge, depending on battery size and wood hardness. A 5.0Ah 56V battery typically delivers 200-300 cuts through 6-inch logs.

Are electric chainsaws safer than gas?

Marginally. They have the same kickback risks, but no hot engine and no fuel hazards. The instant-stop feature (chain stops within 0.5 seconds) is standard on most electrics.

What maintenance do electric chainsaws need?

Much less than gas: keep the chain sharp, maintain proper tension, and keep the bar oiled. No spark plugs, air filters, or fuel system maintenance. Chain sharpening is the same as gas saws.

Electric vs. gas chainsaw — which is better?

For most homeowners, electric wins. Quieter, lighter, instant start, no fuel mixing, less maintenance. Gas still wins for all-day professional use where runtime matters and you can't stop to recharge.
