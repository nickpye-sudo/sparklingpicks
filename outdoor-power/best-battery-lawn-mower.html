Battery Lawn Mower Buying Guide

Choosing the right battery mower depends on your lawn size, existing tool batteries, and features that matter to you.

🔋 Voltage Matters

Higher voltage = more power and longer runtime. Here's how they compare:

  • 40V: Good for lawns under 1/4 acre with normal grass. Lightest and most affordable.
  • 56V–60V: The sweet spot for most suburban lawns (1/4 to 1/2 acre). Matches gas performance.
  • 80V: Maximum power for large yards, thick grass, or challenging terrain. Heaviest.

⏱️ Runtime Reality Check

Manufacturers' runtime claims are best-case scenarios. Expect 70-80% of advertised runtime in real use:

  • 40 min advertised → 30-35 min actual (thick grass, uneven terrain)
  • 60 min advertised → 45-50 min actual

For lawns over 1/4 acre, consider a mower with 50+ min advertised runtime or buy a second battery.

🚗 Push vs. Self-Propelled

Every mower in our top picks is self-propelled — we don't recommend push battery mowers because you're already paying a premium for cordless, and the added weight of batteries makes pushing exhausting.

Self-propelled types:

  • Fixed speed: One speed, simple, reliable
  • Variable speed: Adjustable 1-4 mph, matches your pace (worth the upgrade)
  • Personal Pace (Toro): Auto-adjusts to how hard you push the handle

🔗 Ecosystem Lock-In

Once you pick a battery platform, you'll likely stick with it:

  • Ego 56V: Best ecosystem — mowers, blowers, chainsaws, snow blowers, all excellent
  • Ryobi 40V: Biggest ecosystem — 75+ tools, most affordable
  • DeWalt 20V MAX: Leverage existing power tool batteries, pro-grade
  • Greenworks 60V/80V: Good value, solid selection
  • Kobalt 80V: Lowe's exclusive, powerful but limited ecosystem

💡 Other Features to Consider

  • Multi-blade systems (Ego Select Cut) = finer mulch, better cut quality
  • LED headlights = early morning or evening mowing
  • Height adjustment = single lever vs. wheel-by-wheel (single lever preferred)
  • Smart connectivity = check battery status, but rarely useful in practice

Frequently Asked Questions

Are battery lawn mowers as powerful as gas?

For yards up to 1/2 acre, yes. Modern 56V-80V mowers match or exceed the cutting power of most residential gas mowers. You'll only notice a power difference in extremely thick grass or on yards over 1/2 acre where gas still has an edge in sustained runtime.

How long do lawn mower batteries last?

Expect 3-5 years or 500+ charge cycles before significant capacity loss. Most mowers use lithium-ion cells similar to power tool batteries. Replacement batteries cost $150-300, which is still cheaper than gas mower maintenance over time.

What size lawn can a battery mower handle?

With a single battery: up to 1/4 acre for 40V mowers, up to 1/3 acre for 56V/60V, and up to 1/2 acre for 80V. For larger lawns, buy a second battery or consider a riding mower.

Is Ego or Greenworks better?

Ego has better build quality, cut performance, and ecosystem depth. Greenworks is significantly more affordable with 70-80% of Ego's performance. If budget matters, Greenworks Pro 60V is excellent. If you want the best, Ego is worth the premium.
